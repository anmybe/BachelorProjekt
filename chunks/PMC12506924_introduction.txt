Background and ObjectivesThe significance of anti-contactin–associated protein 1 (Caspr1) antibodies in autoimmune nodopathies (ANs) has not been fully established. The aim of this study was to elucidate the clinical profiles of Caspr1 AN. IntroductionAutoimmune nodopathies (ANs) are relatively newly proposed immune-mediated neuropathies; they are associated with disease-specific autoantibodies against cell adhesion molecules—such as neurofascin (NF) 186, NF155, contactin 1 (CNTN1), and contactin-associated protein 1 (Caspr1)—located in nodes of Ranvier and paranodes.1-4 On the basis of their specific clinicopathologic characteristics, ANs were classified separately from chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP) in the European Academy of Neurology and Peripheral Nerve Society (EAN/PNS) 2021 guidelines.4 Recent cohort studies have established the common clinical features of patients with immunoglobulin (Ig) G4 autoantibodies against NF155 and CNTN1; they are characterized by distal-dominant sensorimotor neuropathy, ataxia, highly elevated CSF protein levels, a poor response to IVIg, and a good response to rituximab.5-12 Moreover, their sural nerve biopsy specimens show axo-glial detachment without macrophage-mediated demyelination, inflammatory cells, or onion bulbs.13Patients with anti-Caspr1 IgG (Caspr1 AN) were first described in 2016, and neuropathic pain and cranial nerve involvement were noted as possible specific features.14,15 However, the clinical profile of Caspr1 AN has not yet been fully elucidated because of its low prevalence and a limited number of case series.16-25 In this study, we retrospectively screened serum samples from the largest cohort of Japanese patients with CIDP to date, aiming to unveil the clinicolaboratory, electrophysiologic, and pathologic features of Caspr1 AN.
