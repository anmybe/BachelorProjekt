Materials and methodsStudy approvalThe clinical trial protocol was approved in Burkina Faso by the Ministry of Health Ethical Committee for Biomedical Research (approval reference N°2021-02-034) and the National Regulatory authority, Comité Technique pour les Essais Cliniques, (approval reference N°2022–02041). The study was conducted according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and International Conference on Harmonization (ICH) Good Clinical Practice (GCP) guidelines. After study approval by competent authorities, the community assent process was initiated, including local community meetings to explain the trial to potentially eligible adults. Community assent was obtained from the local administrative structures, opinion leaders, family heads, and other community members after explanation and discussion of the study. All study participants provided written approved informed consent before any study procedures were performed and for the analysis conducted. The full inclusion and exclusion criteria are available.18Additional details including the full study protocol are available under the initial safety and tolerability publication.18 Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) reporting guidelines22 were included in the prior publication18 and also reported in the analysis conducted within this manuscript. The Clinical Trial was registered in the Pan African Clinical Trials Registry (PACTR202201848463189).Study design and proceduresThe study “Phase 1 Dose Escalating, Double-Blind, Randomised Comparator Controlled Trial of the Safety and Immunogenicity of Different Adjuvant Formulations of R0.6C and ProC6C transmission blocking vaccines candidates against Plasmodium falciparum in Adults in Burkina Faso (TBVax1)” was randomized, staggered, adjuvant-selection, dose-escalation phase 1 clinical trial conducted in the Sabou health district of Burkina Faso in healthy adults, aged 20–45 years. The full study design and protocol are available.18 The analysis reported here focuses on the participants (n = 20 each group) that received either 100 μg ProC6C-AlOH (Statens Serum Institut, Denmark) vaccine supplemented with 50 μg Matrix-MTM adjuvant (Novavax, Sweden) defined as group “G2D” or the placebo Hepatitis B vaccine, Euvax B (LG Chem, The Republic of Korea) defined as group “G2E” (Table 1). Vaccines were administered intramuscularly, three-times, four-weeks apart (D0, D28, D56). The durability endpoint (D140 and D180) samples consisted of n = 20, and n = 16 for G2D, and G2E respectively in this analysis due to loss of follow-up and sample availability.Immunogenicity evaluations of constituent Pfs48/45-6C and Pfs230-pro antigensAntigen-specific IgG antibody levels were determined by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) as previously described.23 In brief, 96-well plates (Nunc Maxisorp Thermo scientific, Cat. No: 456537) were coated with 0.5 μg/mL of Pfs48/45-6C8,24 or Pf230-Pro as appropriate. Antibody concentrations are calculated through regression analysis using TB31F25 monoclonal antibody (mAb; serially diluted 0.2 μg/mL through 0.002 μg/mL) as a reference for antigens which include Pfs48/45-6C and Liberian immune pooled plasma for Pfs230-Pro. Antigen-specific antibodies were detected using horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-conjugated polyclonal Rabbit anti-human IgG (Agilent, cat. No.: P0218), diluted 1:3000 followed by 3,3', 5,5' tetramethylbenzidine dihydrochloride (TMB PLUS2) substrate (Kementec Solutions, Cat. No.: 4395A) and stopped after 20 minutes incubation at room temperature (RT) by adding 100 µl 200 mm H2SO4. Data was processed using Quant Assay for Windows (Version 0.7.1.4) and GraphPad Prism Version 8.3.0.Transmission-reducing activity (standard membrane feeding assay)The transmission-reducing activity (TRA) of samples from volunteers were evaluated using Protein G-purified IgGs at 15 mg/mL, the average physiological concentration of total IgGs in African American adults.26 The assay has been qualified,27 and conducted at National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID). Briefly 16 to 18-day old gametocyte cultures of the P. falciparum NF54 line were mixed with purified IgGs from individual study volunteers and fed to Anopheles stephensi mosquitoes. The insectary was set at 27°C, 75% humidity, and 12 h of light and 12 h of darkness (with 15 minutes of dusk or dawn during the transition), and no additional blood meal was provided after the initial feed. Mosquitos were examined 8 days after feeding to determine oocyst counts (20 mosquitos per group). Experiments were conducted with and without human complement (31% v/v of non-heat inactivated human serum) as noted. The human serum was purchased from Interstate Blood Bank (Memphis, TN). Percent (%) inhibition of mean oocyst intensity (% TRA) was calculated as:100x[1−(mean number of oocysts in the test group)/(mean number of oocysts in the control groups IgGs from malaria naive US serum)] Competition ELISACompetition ELISA was also performed using mAb TB31F25 to measure Pfs48/45 epitope I-specific antibodies. Briefly, ELISA plates (Nunc Maxisorp, Thermo Scientific, Cat. No.: 456537) were coated with TB31F (1 µg/mL) in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) overnight at 4°C. After blocking with PBST (PBS containing 0.01% Tween 20, Sigma-Aldrich, Cat. No.: P7949-500 mL) supplemented with 3% skim milk (Sigma-Aldrich, Cat. No.: 70166-500 G), serially diluted serum samples in PBST containing 1% skim milk and 20 ng/mL Pfs48/45-6C8 were then added to the plates for 1 hour at RT. Serial dilutions of mAb TB31F were also added under identical conditions as a standard. Following the primary incubation, a His-tagged antibody conjugated to HRP (Miltenyi Biotec, Cat. No.: 130-092-783; 1:3000 dilution) was added. The reaction was developed using TMB PLUS2 substrate (Kementec Solutions, Cat. No.: 4395A) for 20 minutes, then stopped by adding 200 mm H₂SO₄. Absorbance was measured at 450 nm. Data were analyzed using Quant Assay for Windows (Version 0.7.1.4) and GraphPad Prism (Version 8.3.0). Pfs48/45 epitope I-specific antibody concentrations were calculated through regression analysis using the TB31F28 mAb as a standard.Statistical analysisThe limit of detection (LOD) for the Pfs48/45-6C and Pfs230-Pro ELISA were 0.04 arbitrary units (a.u.) and 0.75 a.u., respectively. Any values less than the LOD were assigned as half of the LOD values (0.02 and 0.38, respectively) for the analysis. For the comparisons of ELISA data, log-transformed ELISA titers were used for all analyses.To compare data from two groups at the same time point, an unpaired t-test was utilized, while One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s multiple comparisons test were performed for more than two groups. Pairwise comparisons (for ELISA data) were performed using the Paired t test.For SMFA results to determine significant TRA, the best estimate of % inhibition in oocyst density [percentage TRA], and the p values were calculated using a zeroinflated negative binomial (ZINB) random effects model described previously.29 SMFA TRA between group G2D and G2E was completed via Mann–Whitney test, and between D70 and D180 within a group or between with and without complement conditions was by Wilcoxon matched-pairs signed rank test. The correlation between two continuous values was assessed by a Spearman rank test. p values <0.05 were considered significant.R (Version 4.4.1, The R Foundation for Statistical Computing) was used to generate the receiver operating (ROC) curves to assess the classification of performance of the logistic regression with”pROC” R package (version 1.18.5). Optimal cutoff points were determined by Youden analysis (topleft) and with the “OptimalCutpoints” R package (Version 1.1–5). Binary values were assigned to TRA and square root values of IgG concentration utilized. Study participantsThe Phase 1 study of ProC6C was conducted in the Sabou health district in Burkina Faso with the full study details, primary objectives and study protocol published.18 All volunteers received three vaccinations four weeks apart. The present analysis included participants (n = 20 each group) from the 100 µg ProC6C-AlOH/Matrix-M (G2D) and the Hepatitis B (HepB, G2E) control groups alone as prior analysis indicated G2D was the preferred formulation to move forward in clinical development. The demographics of G2D and G2E are reported in Table 1. All samples (n = 20) were available from the ProC6C vaccine group (G2D, Table 1). Due to loss to follow-up (n = 3) or withdrawal of consent (n = 1) 16 of 20 samples (Table 1) were available from the control group (G2E) at D180. Blood samples were taken at baseline (D0), two weeks after each vaccination (D14, D42, D70) and at D140 and D180 to measure durability up to four months post-last dose. Plasmodium falciparum parasite presence was monitored throughout the study and reported (D0, D70, D180) in Table 1.
