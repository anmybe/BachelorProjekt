ResultsComparison of baseline characteristics of participantsIn this study, a total of 12,684 participants were included, comprising 10,266 males (50.8%) and 9,941 females (40.7%), with a mean age of 48.23 ± 17.11 years. Among the participants, 539 were diagnosed with CHD. Baseline characteristics of the participants, weighted/unweighted by mean ± standard deviation or frequency and proportion, are summarized in Tables 1, 2. Significant differences were observed in baseline characteristics, including sex, age, race, education level, income, BMI, blood parameters, antibody status, liver stiffness, liver fat values, and cholesterol levels. Notably, non-Hispanic Whites, participants with higher education levels, and those with negative hepatitis B surface antibody status were more frequently associated with CHD. measured by transient elastography and CHD riskTable 3 displays the association coefficients between liver stiffness and CHD across three progressively adjusted logistic regression models. Liver stiffness, treated as a continuous variable, was categorized into quartiles: Q1 (lowest 25%), Q2 (25–50%), Q3 (50–75%), and Q4 (highest 25%). A consistent positive association was observed between liver stiffness and CHD risk. In the unadjusted model, CHD risk was not significant in Q2 (OR = 1.244; P = 0.160), but it was significant in Q3 (OR = 1.514; P = 0.017) and Q4 (OR = 2.303; P < 0.001). The risk of developing the disease increases in Q3 and Q4 compared to Q1, with corresponding increases in OR values, indicating that the factor is a risk factor.Additionally, Fig. 2 illustrates the RCS regression results for liver stiffness and CHD risk, with the horizontal axis representing liver stiffness and the vertical axis representing the OR values. A clear nonlinear relationship between liver stiffness and CHD risk was observed (P for non-linearity < 0.001). This indicates that as liver stiffness increases, the risk of CHD also rises (Table 4).
