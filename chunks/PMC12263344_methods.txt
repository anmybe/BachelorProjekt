METHODSAfter development and analytical validation of the University of Pittsburgh (“Pitt‐p‐tau217”) method, clinical verification was performed in three independent cohorts (n = 363). 2METHODS2.1Biochemical characterization of p‐tau217 antibodySandwich enzyme‐linked immunosorbent assays (ELISAs) were used to ascertain the specificity of an anti‐p‐tau217 rabbit monoclonal antibody (mAb) from Cell Signaling Technology (clone E9Y4S; #64265). For each measurement, 80 µL of the rabbit mAb at 0.6 µg/mL or the anti‐tau mouse mAb clone Tau5 (epitope: amino acids 210–230 on tau441; BioLegend, #806403) at 1.5 µg/mL, in phosphate‐buffered saline (PBS) pH 7.2 (Gibco, #20012027) was added into the well and incubated overnight at 4°C. The well was then blocked with 200 µL PBS/0.1% bovine serum albumin (Millipore, #81‐053‐3) for 1 hour at room temperature, and washed twice with 300 µL PBST (PBS with 0.1% Tween 20, Bio‐Rad, #1610781). Subsequently, 50 µL of recombinant tau441 (SignalChem, #T08‐54N) or its GSK3beta‐phosphorylated variant (p‐tau441; SignalChem, #T08‐50FN) at concentrations ranging from 0 to 100 ng/mL were added, followed by the addition of 50 µL of PBST with 2% milk (Lab Scientific bioKEMIX, Inc., #978‐907‐4243) and 50 µL of biotinylated anti‐tau mouse mAb, clone Tau12 (directed at tau441 amino acids 6–18; BioLegend, #806502) at 1 µg/mL. Immunocomplex formation proceeded for 1 hour with gentle shaking at 300 rpm on a plate shaker (Fisher Scientific, #88‐861‐023) at room temperature. The wells were washed five times with 300 µL PBST. Streptavidin‐horseradish peroxidase (HRP; Pierce, #21130) was added, and the mixture incubated for 1 hour at room temperature, followed by five washes with 300 µL PBST. The TMB substrate (Thermo Scientific, #34022) was added and allowed to incubate for 30 minutes before stopping the reaction with 100 µL of stop solution (Thermo Scientific, #N600). Epoch SN microplate spectrophotometer (BioTek Instruments, Agilent Technologies, Inc., USA) was used to determine the optical density (OD) at 450 nm with subtraction of the background OD at 550 nm.2.2ImmunohistochemistryAutopsy brain tissue collection and analyses were approved by the University of Pittsburgh Committee for Oversight of Research and Clinical Training Involving Decedents. For immunohistochemistry, brain tissue used was from a 69‐year‐old non‐Hispanic White (NHW) male neuropathologically diagnosed having a high degree of AD neuropathological change (Braak stage VI, high likelihood of AD by the National Institute on Aging–Ronald Reagan Institute criteria 23 , 24 ) with sparse neocortical alpha‐synuclein pathology and no TAR DNA binding protein 43 (TDP‐43) pathology. Post mortem delay was 2 hours. Blocks of tissue from the middle temporal gyrus and from the hippocampus were obtained at autopsy and immediately immersed in ice‐cold 4% paraformaldehyde made in 0.1% sodium phosphate buffer (PB) for 48 hours. Tissue blocks were then sequentially immersed in 15% sucrose and 30% sucrose both made in PB (24 hours each), frozen, and cut into 40 µm thick sections using a freezing, sliding microtome. Tissue sections were then immersed in cryoprotectant solution (1% polyvinylpyrrolidone, 30% sucrose, and 30% ethylene glycol made in PB) and stored at −20°C until used for experiments. Before use, tissue sections were extensively rinsed in PB to remove the cryoprotectant solution. Immunohistochemistry was performed on free‐floating sections as described previously 25 using the VECTASTAIN Elite ABC‐HRP kit (Vector Laboratories, #PK‐6100) with nickel enhanced 30,3‐diaminobenzidine tetra‐hydrochloride (DAB, Sigma, D8001) as the chromogen. The primary antibody (anti‐p‐tau217) was diluted 1:1,000 for use.2.3Plasma p‐tau217 immunoassay development and technical validationAssay development, validation, and clinical studies were performed at the Biofluid Biomarker Laboratory, Department of Psychiatry, School of Medicine, University of Pittsburgh, Pittsburgh, PA, USA. A two‐step ultra‐sensitive p‐tau217 immunoassay was developed on the Simoa HD‐X instrument (Quanterix).RESEARCH IN CONTEXT Systematic review: The authors’ review of PubMed showed that plasma phosphorylated tau (p‐tau)217 evaluation in Alzheimer's disease (AD) has been largely through various immunoassays using an anti‐p‐tau217 antibody from a single source. This study evaluated a new assay using a novel p‐tau217 antibody. Interpretation: The comparable performance of the new versus an established plasma p‐tau217 assay indicates that p‐tau217 is an excellent biomarker regardless of the phosphorylation‐specific antibody used. The Pitt‐p‐tau217 assay exhibited strong diagnostic accuracy in a cohort of autosomal dominant AD participants with blood‐to‐autopsy data, as well as in population‐based groups of older adults. We established a foundation for widening participation in high‐performing plasma p‐tau217 assay development in academic laboratories. Future directions: Our results provide novel reagents and a validated assay that could spur the development of more cost‐effective and accessible methods to detect early AD in research and diagnostic settings. For capture, the rabbit p‐tau217 mAb was coupled to paramagnetic beads (Quanterix, #103207). Biotin‐conjugated mouse mAb raised against the N‐terminal region of tau (Tau12; BioLegend, #SIG‐39416) was used for detection. In vitro phosphorylated recombinant full‐length tau441 (SignalChem, #T08‐50FN‐50) was used as the assay calibrator. Plasma samples and calibrators were diluted with assay diluent (sample diluent, Quanterix, #104101). Optimal required dilution of samples was established as 1:4, as at this dilution measured samples gave signals around the middle of the standard curve.Analytical validation focused on dilution linearity, competitive binding, day‐to‐day stability, and spike recovery was done as described previously. 26 Sensitivity (lower limit of quantification [LLOQ]) was calculated from a eight‐point standard curve using recombinant p‐tau441 (0–96 pg/mL; in duplicate). These curves were generated on two distinct HD‐X instruments, using different lots of antibody‐conjugated paramagnetic beads, each prepared on a separate day. For assay specificity, a p‐tau217‐containing antigen (synthetic peptide sourced from Sino Biologicals US Inc.) at varying concentrations of 0.1–200 µg/mL was used for competitive binding with endogenous p‐tau217 in plasma samples. In brief, p‐tau217‐containing antigen peptide concentrations ranging from 0.1 to 200 µg/mL were added to plasma samples, followed by 4X dilutions with assay diluent, and then analyzed using the Pitt‐p‐tau217 assay.2.4Biomarker clinical studiesIn the clinical studies, p‐tau217 was measured in 1:4 dilution of plasma samples by scientists blinded to participant information. Plasma samples were analyzed in singlicate while signal variations within and between analytical runs were monitored using three quality controls (QC) samples measured in duplicate at the start and the end of each technical run.For comparative purposes, plasma p‐tau217 was also measured with a commercial method from Quanterix, Inc. (ALZpath‐p‐tau217 assay kit #103714) using the Simoa HD‐X platform for a selection of cohorts.2.5Cohorts2.5.1ADAD studyThis cohort included plasma samples from individuals who underwent brain autopsies conducted by the Neuropathology Core of the University of Pittsburgh Alzheimer's Disease Research Center (ADRC). The neuropathological diagnosis was determined as described previously, using the Consortium to Establish a Registry for Alzheimer's Disease (CERAD) criteria, 27 Braak neurofibrillary tangle staging, 28 and Thal amyloid beta (Aβ) phases. 29 This resulted in a composite ABC score for each case according to the National Institute on Aging–Alzheimer's Association criteria. 23 The clinical evaluation for diagnosing AD dementia adhered to established criteria. Subgroups were identified with neurochemically defined AD and AD dementia. Of these, some had sporadic AD and others ADAD. Five sporadic AD and two ADAD participants underwent [11C]Pittsburgh compound B (PiB) positron emission tomography (PET) imaging for Aβ and later came to autopsy. Five out of six cases classified as ADAD were confirmed to have a defined presenilin 1 (PSEN1) mutation through genetic testing. Control participants, matched in age and sex, without AD profiles as determined by clinical evaluation, were selected from the ADRC for comparison.2.5.2Monongahela–Youghiogheny Healthy Aging Team‐Neuroimaging StudyThe Monongahela–Youghiogheny Healthy Aging Team‐Neuroimaging (MYHAT‐NI) is a substudy of the parent MYHAT study, which is a population‐based research cohort that focuses on selected communities from a Rust Belt region of southwestern Pennsylvania, USA. The participants were selected through age‐stratified random sampling from the publicly available voter registration lists during two time periods: 2006 to 2008 and 2016 to 2019. The eligibility criteria upon enrollment encompassed: (1) being aged ≥ 65 years, (2) residing in a specified town, (3) not living in long‐term care facilities, (4) possessing adequate hearing and vision for completing neuropsychological assessments, and (5) being able to make decisions. Participants were categorized as cognitively normal if they scored 0 on the Clinical Dementia Rating global score (CDR), classified as mildly cognitively impaired with a CDR of 0.5, and diagnosed with dementia if their CDR was ≥ 1. Aβ PET was assessed using [11C]PiB and tau PET used [18F]MK‐6240.2.5.3Human Connectome Project cohortThe Human Connectome Project (HCP) cohort is an ongoing longitudinal, community‐based study in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, USA, examining brain structural and functional connectivity among individuals aged 56 to 79 years, including both cognitively normal and cognitively impaired participants. The HCP study uses the Pittsburgh ADRC neuropsychological test battery assessments (including the Montreal Cognitive Assessment [MoCA], verbal fluency, a 30‐item visual naming test, Trail Making Test verbal free recall, and the Rey‐Osterrieth Complex Figure), along with brain structural and functional imaging techniques such as functional magnetic resonance imaging [MRI] and magnetoencephalography to evaluate brain connectivity. Additionally, [11C]PiB PET imaging was conducted in vivo to examine brain Aβ pathology. Participants were classified into normal, impaired without complaints, subjective cognitive complaints, mild cognitive impairment (MCI, both amnestic and non‐amnestic), and AD using the ADRC classification scheme. 30 In the MYHAT‐NI and HCP cohorts, we used previously documented approaches to determine Aβ PET positivity standardized uptake value ratio (SUVR) > 1.346. 31 , 32 Aβ PET SUVR values were converted to the Centiloid (CL) scale 33 using established cutoff values: Aβ negative (CL < 15), low‐burden Aβ (CL 15–25), and Aβ positive (CL > 25).2.6Ethical clearanceThe University of Pittsburgh Institutional Review Board approved the study procedures, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants. The reference numbers for the study approvals are as follows: ADAD study, MOD19110245‐023; MYHAT‐NI, STUDY19020264; and HCP, STUDY19100015C.2.7Statistical analysesContinuous data are shown as median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables are reported as counts (%). Group differences in continuous variables were assessed using the Wilcoxon rank sum test (for two categories) or the Kruskal–Wallis test with Dunn multiple comparisons (for three or more groups), with p values adjusted using Bonferroni correction. Likewise, group differences in categorical variables were assessed using Fisher exact test. The overall association between biomarker pairs within each study was reported using Spearman correlation. Multivariable logistic regression models were built to assess the performance of each p‐tau217 assay in predicting Aβ PET and tau PET status. The primary predictor of these models was the biomarker of interest. Additionally, the models were adjusted for age, sex, and apolipoprotein E (APOE) ε4 carrier status as covariates. Models were fit for each biomarker and each study separately. Performance was assessed using 3‐fold cross‐validation, in which the data are trained on two thirds of the data and tested on the remaining one third that was not used in model building. This process was repeated three times, once for each of the “left out” folds. For MYHAT‐NI and HCP cohorts diagnostic potential was examined using receiver operating characteristic curves and area under the curve (AUC‐ROC). The reported AUC is the average of the test set AUCs across folds. The AUCs from different biomarkers were compared by examining the overlap in their confidence intervals, estimated using the method proposed previously. 34 Python (version 3.12) was used to measure AUC‐ROC for ADAD study. Throughout the analyses, statistical significance was set at p < 0.05. Three data points, one from the ADAD study and two from the HCP cohort, that fell outside the calibration curve range were excluded from the final analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using R (version 4.4.2) and Python, and plots were generated using Python, R, or GraphPad Prism (version 10.1.1). 3.5ParticipantsWe included a total of 363 participants from three independent cohorts.The ADAD cohort (n = 21) included autopsy‐verified individuals with sporadic AD (n = 8) and ADAD due to PSEN1 mutations (n = 6) as well as non‐AD controls (n = 7).
