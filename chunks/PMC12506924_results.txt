ResultsOf 559 screened serum samples from patients with CIDP (median age at onset 54 years, 35% female), 19 cases with anti-Caspr1 IgG were identified; the main subclass was IgG4 (17 cases). Patients with IgG4 Caspr1 AN cases exhibited an older age at disease onset (median 67 years); male predominance (82%); high proportions of limb weakness (100%), gait disturbance (100%), tremor (65%), and sensory ataxia (82%); and very high CSF protein levels (249 mg/dL). Nerve conduction studies showed prolonged F-wave and distal motor latencies and reduced sensorimotor conduction velocities in all nerves tested. Light microscopy findings of sural nerve biopsy specimens from 4 patients with Caspr1 AN indicated a loss of myelinated fiber density and myelin ovoids without macrophage-mediated demyelination or onion bulbs. Electron microscopic evaluation demonstrated axo-glial detachment. Patients with IgG4 Caspr1 AN showed a poor response to IVIg (31%) and needed combined immunotherapies. Even after correcting for age, serum NfL levels were higher in patients with IgG4 Caspr1 AN than in healthy controls (p = 0.0609) and correlated with INCAT scores (p = 0.0143). In the 7 patients with 2 consecutive serum samples, antibody titers decreased with clinical improvement. Demographic data of patients with CIDP are summarized in eTable 1. Twenty-four patients were seropositive for autoantibodies; none of the healthy (n = 32) or disease (n = 79) controls showed reactivity against Caspr1 (Figure 2A). Anti-CNTN1 IgG was also screened using live CBA, and 23 cases were identified (Figure 1), of which the main subclass was IgG4 in 21 cases and non-IgG4 in 2 cases. Thus, we identified 19 Caspr1 AN—17 with IgG4 subclass predominance—and 1 Caspr1 and CNTN1 AN.Clinical and Electrophysiologic Features of Caspr1 ANTable 1 provides the clinical and laboratory features of Caspr1 AN. Of the 17 with IgG4 Caspr1 AN, 13 and 4 cases were categorized into typical and distal phenotypes, respectively. All patients had symmetric 4-limb weakness, hypo/areflexia, and disturbance of deep sensation. Limb weakness was distal or distal-dominant in 12 of the 17 patients (70.6%). Patients with IgG4 Caspr1 AN exhibited an older age at disease onset (67.0 years) and high proportions of gait disturbance (100%), tremor (64.7%), and sensory ataxia (82.4%). Cranial nerve involvement was observed in 5 patients (29.4%), including facial nerve palsy (n = 3), hypogeusia (n = 1), hoarse voice (n = 1), dysarthria (n = 1), and dysphagia (n = 1) (eTable 2). CNS symptoms were observed in 2 patients: one with cerebellar involvement such as slurred speech, nystagmus, and a combination of sensory and cerebellar ataxia and the other with involuntary movements and memory impairments.28 Seven patients (41.2%) showed autonomic symptoms such as constipation (n = 6), frequent urination (n = 1), dysuria (n = 1), and orthostatic dizziness (n = 1). Four of the 17 patients (23.5%) had diabetes mellitus. In addition, high CSF protein levels (249.4 mg/dL) were observed. No examined patients showed intracerebral lesions strongly suggesting demyelination on brain MRI. Magnetic resonance neurography detected symmetrically mild-to-moderate enlargement of the cervical and lumbosacral roots in 76.9% and 68.8%, respectively, of patients (eFigure 3). In all nerves tested, NCSs showed prolonged F-wave and distal motor latencies and reduced motor nerve conduction velocities (Table 2).
