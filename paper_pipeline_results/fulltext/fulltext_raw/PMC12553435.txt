Background
In this post hoc analysis of the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES (Evaluation of Cardiovascular Outcomes After an Acute Coronary Syndrome During Treatment With Alirocumab) trial, we evaluated the efficacy of alirocumab in patients with probable heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) or type III hyperlipoproteinemia (T3HLP).
Methods
Patients had clinical HeFH if baseline low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol was ≥250 mg/dL and the qualifying acute coronary syndrome event occurred before age 55 (men) or 60 years (women), or if baseline low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol was ≥330 mg/dL. Patients had T3HLP if the ratio, validated using the UK Biobank database, of non–high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol/apolipoprotein B was >2.6. Genetic variation affecting low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol levels (HeFH) and
APOE
genotypes (T3HLP) was assessed in a pharmacogenomics subgroup.
Results
In total, 5.8% of patients from ODYSSEY OUTCOMES and 5.9% from the pharmacogenomics subgroup qualified as having clinical HeFH. In the pharmacogenomics subgroup, 295 patients (2.5%) had genetically confirmed HeFH; 45.8% also qualified as having clinical HeFH. Lipid responses and clinical benefits of alirocumab were similar in all patients with or without HeFH. In the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES cohort, 30 of 18 924 patients (0.16%) were identified as having T3HLP. The pharmacogenomics subgroup included 13 of these 30 patients, of whom 7 (53.8%) were
APOE2/2
homozygotes. Lipid responses to alirocumab in patients with T3HLP were comparable with those in the general population. In those with genetic dyslipidemia, adverse event incidence was similar to the overall group.
Conclusions
In ODYSSEY OUTCOMES, individuals with HeFH were common while those with T3HLP were rare. In their lipid responses, carriers of both conditions benefited from alirocumab.

Clinical Perspective
What Is New?
Our results demonstrate that alirocumab, a proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 monoclonal antibody, markedly reduces atherogenic lipid levels; our results also suggest reducing cardiovascular risk in patients with probable heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia or type III hyperlipoproteinemia.
What Are the Clinical Implications?
Marked reductions in atherogenic lipids were also seen in patients with type III hyperlipoproteinemia treated with alirocumab versus placebo.
Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 monoclonal antibodies are already indicated for use in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, but their use and utility in patients with type III hyperlipoproteinemia should also be recognized.
PCSK9 (Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9) inhibiting monoclonal antibodies, which increase expression of the low‐density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR), effectively reduce low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‐C) levels even in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH), whose LDLR function is diminished.

,
2
However, while outcome trials that confirmed the clinical benefits of the 2 available antibodies did not specifically target patients with HeFH, it is likely that many participants had this condition given the study entry requirement (LDL‐C >70 mg/dL on maximally tolerated, high‐potency lipid‐lowering therapy [LLT] and preexisting coronary artery disease).
3

Several useful diagnostic criteria have been developed for the clinical diagnosis of familial hypercholesterolemia (FH).
5
,
6
,
7
One of the main tools was developed by the Dutch Lipid Clinic Network (DLCN), which uses a scoring system (3–5, possible FH; 6–8, probable FH; and >8, definite FH) to predict the likelihood of FH in the absence of genetic information.
5
Because large trials do not usually collect much of the information needed for the DLCN score (ie, xanthomas, arcus, detailed family history, or genetic data), retrospective attribution of FH status must rely on the presence of LDL‐C levels (≥250–330 mg/dL [adds 5 points]; ≥330 mg/dL [adds 8 points]),
7
and the presence of premature coronary artery disease (adds 2 points). While it is difficult to extract off‐treatment data in those who have been receiving long‐term statin therapy, methods to back‐calculate off‐treatment LDL‐C levels based on type and dose of statin have been used and validated.
8

Just like HeFH, T3HLP is more rigorously and accurately diagnosed with a genetic test but, unlike HeFH, its genotype only rarely produces dyslipidemia (because of low penetrance), and its phenotype is not easily identified solely on the basis of the standard lipid panel (often diagnosed as common combined dyslipidemia). The ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trial was a phase 3, randomized, placebo‐controlled, multicenter study designed to compare the efficacy of alirocumab versus placebo on cardiovascular outcomes among patients with recent acute coronary syndrome (ACS) on intensive or maximally tolerated statin therapy.
4
A total of 18 924 patients were randomized, and the study met the primary end point of a 15% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) in the treatment group versus placebo (hazard ratio [HR], 0.85 [95% CI, 0.73–0.98]).
In this post hoc analysis, we used established and novel biomarkers to identify patients from the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trial who may have been affected by HeFH or T3HLP, and to determine the efficacy of alirocumab on their lipid parameters and, for HeFH, cardiovascular outcomes.

Study Design and Participants
The primary efficacy and safety results for the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trial have been published.
4
In brief, the trial consisted of patients aged ≥40 years who had been hospitalized with ACS 1 to 12 months before randomization and had LDL‐C levels ≥70 mg/dL (1.81 mmol/L), non–high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL‐C) levels ≥100 mg/dL (2.59 mmol/L), or apolipoprotein B (Apo B) levels ≥80 mg/dL after at least 2 weeks of stable LLTs. Patients were randomly assigned (1:1) to blinded treatment with subcutaneous alirocumab 75 mg or matching placebo every 2 weeks. The dose of alirocumab was adjusted under blinded conditions to target an LDL‐C level of 25–50 mg/dL (0.6–1.3 mmol/L).
HeFH
Study Population
To identify patients with HeFH in the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trial, the DLCN criteria were used.
5
As most patients were already on statins, the untreated LDL‐C concentration was calculated using the adjustment factor provided in the supporting documentation for the DLCN tool.
5
,
22
Untreated LDL‐C was calculated using LLT‐specific adjustment factors with a formula that was previously validated.
8
,
23
Back‐calculated, untreated LDL‐C and the presence of early‐onset coronary artery disease (age <55 years in men and <60 years in women) were the 2 criteria used to assess the DLCN score for each participant. We defined an individual as having HeFH if the DLCN score categorized them as having at least probable HeFH (DLCN score ≥6).

Assessments
Baseline characteristics for patients with HeFH and T3HLP were assessed. Changes in LDL‐C, HDL‐C, triglycerides, total cholesterol, and Apo B after treatment with alirocumab were evaluated from baseline to month 48. Changes in lipoprotein (a) after treatment with alirocumab were evaluated from baseline to month 12. MACEs (defined as the composite of death from coronary heart disease, nonfatal myocardial infarction, fatal or nonfatal ischemic stroke, and unstable angina requiring hospitalization) after treatment with alirocumab were assessed in patients with HeFH and T3HLP.
Statistical Analysis
Continuous variables were summarized using mean±SD or median (interquartile range). For categorical or ordinal variables, frequencies and percentages were provided. Baseline characteristics were summarized by HeFH and T3HLP status. Additionally, changes in LDL‐C, HDL‐C, triglycerides, total cholesterol, Apo B, and lipoprotein (a) were summarized using descriptive statistics. Efficacy data were summarized by treatment arms for MACE outcomes in subgroups of HeFH and T3HLP. The proportion difference and 95% CIs were reported for descriptive purposes to make within‐group comparisons in patients with and without HeFH. Treatment HRs and 95% CIs were estimated using a Cox proportional hazard model, stratified by geographic region. A
P
value for interaction was calculated to assess the heterogeneity of treatment differences in subgroups by HeFH status. The proportional hazard assumptions were checked via the Schoenfeld test, and the influential observations were examined through the “difference in the estimates of β” values. The cumulative incidence of MACEs over time was estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods by treatment group. Due to the low sample size of patients with T3HLP, we provided only the incidence for descriptive comparisons by treatment arm and T3HLP status. In the genetic analyses, a linear regression model adjusting for age, sex, and 10 principal components were used to evaluate the relationship between the LDL‐C PRS and LLT‐adjusted (using the DLCN documentation noted above) baseline LDL‐C measures. All analyses were done in SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC), plink 1.9/2.0, and R 4.1.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Discussion
We investigated the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES database to determine the lipid and clinical responses to alirocumab treatment in 2 groups of patients likely to carry genetic dyslipidemias (HeFH or T3HLP) and increased risk of cardiovascular disease (CVD) events due to lifelong exposure to dyslipidemia. The importance of lifetime exposure to high levels of LDL‐C is corroborated by our results, which show similar LDL‐C levels but higher CVD rates among patients with HeFH treated with alirocumab compared with patients without HeFH receiving placebo. Using clinical biomarkers, we determined that ≈6% of the total trial population could be classified as having probable HeFH. This is consistent with the entry criteria for this trial (elevated LDL‐C on maximally tolerated oral therapy and history of coronary artery disease). In contrast, only 30 patients with T3HLP were identified. This is also consistent with expectations because this condition is rare, recessive, and with low penetrance. Treatment with alirocumab decreased LDL‐C, non–HDL‐C, and Apo B levels in patients with HeFH or T3HLP to a similar extent compared with the overall study population. This is the first time that effective lipid lowering in patients with T3HLP has been shown with alirocumab or any PCSK9 inhibitor. In addition, patients with HeFH showed similar treatment HR for MACEs compared with the overall study population, although the absolute reduction in risk of MACE was numerically greater in the former group.
Our results provide additional support for the concept that individuals with ACS and hyperlipidemia may have unrecognized genetic dyslipidemias, conferring a higher risk of CVD events, and that these individuals are responsive to effective LLTs. The younger age of individuals with versus without HeFH in this study, together with the increased rate of MACEs in those with HeFH, highlights the need for early diagnosis of this condition. Our data show an overall modest concordance between genetic and clinical diagnosis of HeFH, with a number needed to test to find a genetically confirmed patient with HeFH of ≈40. These results suggest that genetic testing in patients with post‐ACS with elevated LDL‐C may be cost effective. Furthermore, given the relatively low cost of genetic testing in the United States, the benefit of cascade screening in HeFH is potentially very large. Patients with HeFH have been extensively investigated and shown to respond to PCSK9 inhibitor monoclonal antibodies,

This study includes limitations inherent to post hoc analyses. Other limitations include back‐calculated LDL‐C levels to derive HeFH diagnosis and a smaller pharmacogenomics subgroup, making it difficult to draw inferences about possible CVD risk reduction in patients with T3HLP. In addition, the phenotypic definition of T3HLP did not account for statin use. Moreover, the DLCN scoring process did not include family history data, as these did not include information on age at the time of event. Additionally, at entry into the trial, case report forms did not collect information regarding a prior clinical or genetic diagnosis of HeFH. Therefore, we do not know the proportion of patients with genetically proven or clinically presumed HeFH. The lack of interaction with/inability to inform participants and their relatives during this post hoc analysis was a missed opportunity and a lesson for future trials. Finally, it was assumed the T3HLP biomarker ratio is constant when untreated patients are taking high‐intensity statins.
In conclusion, our study shows that patients with HeFH, identified using established criteria, appear to benefit from PCSK9 inhibition similarly to patients with common hypercholesterolemia in terms of lipid responses and clinical outcomes. In addition, patients with T3HLP, identified using a biomarker ratio validated using the UK Biobank database, also benefit from PCSK9 inhibition similarly to patients with common dyslipidemia in terms of lipid responses.
