BIOMARKER PROFILE
==============================

Biomarker
---------
Platelet-to-Lymphocyte Ratio (PLR)

Biomarker Group
---------------
inflammatory_changes

Relevance for Sports
--------------------
7 / 10

Activity Context
----------------
Studies involved: (1) Elite male soccer players during 12 weeks, encompassing 6 weeks of uncongested match play (30 training sessions, 6 matches) and 6 weeks of congested match play (24 training sessions, 10 matches). (2) Endurance athletes performing two identical 60-minute continuous endurance running field tests at high intensity (95% HR at IAT for 40 min, 110% HR at IAT for 20 min) separated by a four-week recovery period. (3) Middle-aged amateur long-distance runners undergoing 6-week daily curcumin supplementation alongside regular endurance training and an incremental treadmill running test.

Effect Summary
--------------
PLR showed mixed responses to athletic activity. In elite soccer players, it was significantly higher during a congested match play period compared to uncongested periods. After the congested period, higher PLR was negatively correlated with aerobic physical fitness (YYIRL1 performance and VO2max). In contrast, a study on endurance athletes undergoing strenuous 60-minute running tests found no significant changes in PLR over the exercise and recovery time points, though it demonstrated moderate reliability (ICC 0.51–0.74). In amateur long-distance runners, PLR exhibited varied significant changes across time points within both curcumin and placebo groups following intense exercise. Specifically, the placebo group showed a significantly lower resting PLR in the second trial, and the curcumin group exhibited a significantly lower PLR at 1 hour post-test compared to placebo in both trials.

Sport Implications
------------------
PLR shows potential utility for monitoring training intensity, competitive load, and aerobic physical fitness, particularly in sports with periods of high match congestion like elite soccer. Elevated PLR in congested periods may indicate increased systemic inflammatory processes and exercise-induced strain, which could negatively impact physical fitness and potentially signal a risk of overtraining or underrecovery. Conversely, an unchanged PLR alongside improved performance might suggest effective training adaptation without excessive inflammatory burden. However, its responsiveness appears context-dependent; PLR did not show significant changes after acute strenuous endurance exercise, questioning its suitability as a robust biomarker for acute exercise response in this context. While curcumin supplementation led to a lower PLR post-exercise in one study, the overall reliability of PLR as a clear diagnostic marker for exercise-induced inflammation was not strongly supported by these findings.
