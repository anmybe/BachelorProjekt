Relevance for Sports

Activity Context
----------------
The study involved participants engaged in repetitive prolonged moderate walking exercise over 3 consecutive days (e.g., Nijmegen 4 Day Marches), covering 30, 40, or 50 km/day at a self-selected pace. Exercise intensity on day 1 averaged 65% of maximum heart rate. This represents a sustained endurance training load typical of multi-day events or an overreaching training phase for endurance athletes.

Effect Summary
--------------
CD16 bright /CD62L bright neutrophils in peripheral blood decreased significantly from 4.58 (3.33–5.70) × 10^6 /ml at day 1 to 3.12 (2.41–4.57) × 10^6 /ml after day 2 of prolonged walking. This decrease was identified as the main reason for the overall decrease in total neutrophil count during the sustained exercise period.

Sport Implications
------------------
The observed decrease in CD16 bright /CD62L bright neutrophils signifies a shift in specific neutrophil populations and contributes to the dynamic immune response to sustained, multi-day endurance exercise. Monitoring changes in these specific neutrophil subsets can help characterize the immune system's adaptation or response to prolonged training loads. While the overall study suggests that increased neutrophil activation (marked by high CD11b) is associated with impaired physical feeling and physiological stress, the specific decrease in CD16 bright /CD62L bright neutrophils reflects a key component of the innate immune system's reaction and potential adaptation to cumulative exercise stress, contributing to the broader understanding of training load monitoring, recovery, and potential under-recovery or overtraining states by indicating immune system modulation.
