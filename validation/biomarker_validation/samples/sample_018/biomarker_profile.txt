BIOMARKER PROFILE
==============================

Biomarker
---------
P100 Torque

Biomarker Group
---------------
muscle_damage

Relevance for Sports
--------------------
9 / 10

Activity Context
----------------
The study involved healthy males performing 50 intermittent drop jumps from approximately 0.5 m height. This exercise protocol was designed to be mechanically demanding, inducing muscle damage without significant metabolic stress. No specific athlete population or training state was reported beyond 'healthy males'.

Effect Summary
--------------
P100 torque, an indicator of muscle contractile properties and fatigue measured by high-frequency electrically evoked muscle torque, significantly decreased immediately after 50 drop jumps (to 79.5% ± 15.4% of baseline). It was restored to near baseline levels within 12 hours post-exercise (91.9% ± 10.6%). The decrease in P100 torque correlated with increased cell-free DNA (cfDNA) levels (r = -0.3, P = 0.00038), which is a marker of primary muscle damage.

Sport Implications
------------------
P100 torque serves as a direct measure of muscle function, specifically reflecting high-frequency contractile fatigue and the acute loss of muscle force following mechanically demanding exercise. Its significant decrease post-drop jumps indicates acute muscle damage and functional impairment, aligning with muscle damage and repair processes. The relatively quick restoration within 12 hours provides insight into immediate recovery timelines for specific muscle function after such stressors. For athletes, monitoring P100 torque could be a valuable performance diagnostic tool for assessing acute muscle fatigue and recovery status following plyometric or eccentric-loading sessions. Persistent deficits in P100 torque beyond expected recovery periods could signal inadequate recovery, increased training load stress, or potential overreaching, thereby informing training load adjustments and strategies to optimize adaptation and prevent underrecovery.
