BIOMARKER PROFILE
==============================

Biomarker
---------
Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Receptor 1

Biomarker Group
---------------
cardio_risk

Relevance for Sports
--------------------
7 / 10

Activity Context
----------------
The study involved 75 middle-aged adults. Interventions included acute symptom-limited treadmill exercise (Bruce protocol, up to 90% predicted peak heart rate, 9.6 METs) and a 20-week chronic aerobic exercise training program (three sessions/week, intensity and duration gradually increased). The training state was a general population undergoing exercise, not specifically trained athletes.

Effect Summary
--------------
Plasma Vascular Endothelial Growth Factor Receptor 1 (FLT1) showed higher abundance correlating with higher metabolic equivalents (METs) achieved at peak acute treadmill exercise. It was identified as an exercise-responsive protein.

Sport Implications
------------------
As an exercise-responsive protein, FLT1 levels correlating with exercise capacity (METs) suggest it could potentially serve as a marker reflecting the acute cardiovascular and vascular endothelial response to exercise. In a sports context, understanding such responses is critical for monitoring training load and adaptation, particularly concerning cardiovascular adaptations like angiogenesis and vascular remodeling which underpin improved endurance performance and exercise tolerance. While the study population was middle-aged adults, the principle of FLT1's responsiveness to exercise and its association with exercise capacity indicates its potential utility in assessing the physiological strain and cardiovascular adaptations induced by training in athletic populations. Further research in athletes is needed to confirm its specific role in training load monitoring, recovery, or performance diagnostics in this demographic.
