Biomarker

Individual OFF-hour Score

Biomarker Group
---------------

Relevance for Sports
--------------------

Activity Context
----------------
Forty-eight healthy volunteers were orally administered one of six selected Chinese herbal medicine (CHM) products for two weeks. The products included five single herbs (Angelicae Sinensis Radix (ASR), Astragali Radix (AR), Salviae Miltiorrhizae Radix et Rhizoma (SMR), Asini Corii Colla (ACC), Spatholobi Caulis (SC)) at 3.6 g/day, and one formula (Gui-Pi-Tang (GPT)) at 20 g/day. This study does not report on an athlete population or any specific training state; the subjects were healthy volunteers.

Effect Summary
--------------
The Individual OFF-hour Score (OFFS), a calculated haematological variable, significantly decreased during a 2-week intervention with Angelicae Sinensis Radix (ASR), Astragali Radix (AR), and Salviae Miltiorrhizae Radix et Rhizoma (SMR) in healthy volunteers, and this decrease sustained for a period after withdrawal. Specifically, ASR decreased OFFS from 98.8 to 68.1-68.5, AR declined from 95.6 to 61.1-68.4, and SMR decreased from 96.3 to 59.3-65.3. This decrease in OFFS is associated with increased reticulocyte percentage (RET%), suggesting enhanced erythropoiesis.

Sport Implications
------------------
The Individual OFF-hour Score (OFFS) is a critical component of the Athlete Biological Passport (ABP) Haematological Module, designed to detect erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) or blood doping. A significant decrease in OFFS, particularly when accompanied by an increase in Reticulocyte percentage (RET%), indicates enhanced erythropoiesis. This can trigger an Atypical Passport Finding (ATPF), signaling potential manipulation of red blood cell mass. From a sports science perspective, monitoring OFFS is primarily a tool for anti-doping surveillance rather than directly assessing training stress, adaptation, performance, or recovery in a natural physiological context. However, substances or interventions that influence erythropoiesis, leading to changes in OFFS, could indirectly affect an athlete's oxygen carrying capacity and endurance performance, regardless of whether they are permissible. Therefore, understanding factors that alter OFFS is crucial for interpreting ABP data and managing athlete health and anti-doping compliance, even if the primary study context was healthy volunteers taking CHMs.
