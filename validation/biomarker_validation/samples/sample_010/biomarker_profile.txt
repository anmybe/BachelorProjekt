Activity Context
----------------
This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluates the effects of soy protein (SP), soy foods, or isoflavone extracts supplementation on various physiological parameters in active individuals and athletes. Supplementation protocols varied widely in dose (acute to chronic daily intakes from 0.5 g/kg BW/day to 90 g/day, often combined with carbohydrates) and duration (single sessions to 12 weeks). Comparisons were made against whey protein, soy-dairy blends, isoenergetic placebos, or control groups. The context is generally related to muscle adaptation, metabolic status, antioxidant capacity, hormonal response, and exercise performance.

Effect Summary
--------------
A 41-study meta-analysis reported no significant effect of soy protein, soy foods, or isoflavone extracts supplementation on estrone levels.

Sport Implications
------------------
Estrone is a weaker estrogen, primarily produced in fat and muscle tissue. The finding that soy protein, soy foods, or isoflavone extracts supplementation has no significant effect on estrone levels in active individuals and athletes addresses a common concern regarding the potential feminizing effects or adverse hormonal shifts associated with soy consumption in an athletic population. This suggests that athletes utilizing soy protein as part of their nutritional strategy for muscle adaptation, recovery, or performance enhancement need not be concerned about changes in estrone impacting their endocrine profile or training adaptations.
