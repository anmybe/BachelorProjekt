BIOMARKER PROFILE
==============================

Biomarker
---------
Protein Carbonyls

Biomarker Group
---------------
cell_damage

Relevance for Sports
--------------------
8 / 10

Activity Context
----------------
This biomarker was investigated in various athletic contexts involving strenuous exercise and recovery interventions. These include men performing intense resistance exercise after tart cherry supplementation, high-level kayakers undergoing specialized training combined with whole-body cryotherapy, female team-sport athletes recovering from repeated simulated team games with either cow's milk or a carbohydrate solution, and active individuals/athletes undertaking speed-endurance exercise with soy protein, whey protein, or placebo supplementation.

Effect Summary
--------------
Protein Carbonyls were significantly lower or recovered faster with tart cherry extract following intense resistance exercise (nullifying the increase seen in placebo). Similarly, soy protein supplementation tended to result in lower levels or faster recovery of Protein Carbonyls compared to whey protein or placebo after speed-endurance exercise. However, no statistically significant changes were observed during a training cycle involving combined physical exercise and whole-body cryotherapy in kayakers, nor were there clear effects in female team-sport athletes recovering with milk versus carbohydrate solutions after simulated team games.

Sport Implications
------------------
Protein Carbonyls serve as an indicator of oxidative stress and protein damage, a common consequence of intense athletic training. The observed reductions or faster recovery of Protein Carbonyls with tart cherry extract and soy protein supplementation suggest a protective effect against exercise-induced oxidative stress. This protective mechanism may contribute to improved recovery and adaptation by mitigating cellular damage, which is crucial for maintaining muscle function and overall physiological integrity. Monitoring Protein Carbonyls could, therefore, aid in assessing the acute training stress, evaluating the effectiveness of recovery strategies (e.g., specific nutritional interventions), and potentially informing adjustments to training load to optimize the balance between stress and adaptation for athletes. The absence of change in some contexts indicates that either the intervention was not impactful on protein oxidation or the exercise stimulus was insufficient to elicit a measurable change in this specific biomarker.
