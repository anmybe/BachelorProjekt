BIOMARKER PROFILE
==============================

Biomarker
---------
Whole Blood Viscosity (High-Shear Rate / Systolic)

Biomarker Group
---------------
cardio_risk

Relevance for Sports
--------------------
8 / 10

Activity Context
----------------
Healthy adults performed moderate exercise in a warm environment (30 °C, 70% relative humidity) to induce mild dehydration (2.0 ± 0.2% body weight loss). Following exercise, participants underwent a 2-hour rehydration and recovery period, consuming either electrolyzed high-pH alkaline water or standard purified water in a volume equivalent to 2% of their pre-exercise body weight.

Effect Summary
--------------
Rehydration with high-pH alkaline water significantly reduced high-shear whole blood viscosity and accelerated its restoration to pre-exercise baseline compared to standard purified water. Specifically, high-pH alkaline water reduced viscosity by an average of 6.30% vs. 3.36% with standard purified water. Mean systolic viscosity levels for subjects consuming high-pH alkaline water returned to pre-exercise baseline within 60 minutes, whereas those consuming standard purified water did not return within 120 minutes.

Sport Implications
------------------
Whole blood viscosity is a critical indicator of cardiovascular function and oxygen delivery, reflecting intravascular hydration status. Post-exercise, increased blood viscosity signifies greater cardiovascular strain and potentially impaired oxygen transport to working muscles. The observed faster reduction and restoration of blood viscosity to baseline with high-pH alkaline water suggests enhanced rehydration efficiency and potentially accelerated cardiovascular recovery from exercise-induced dehydration. This could lead to improved athletic performance by optimizing blood flow, oxygen delivery, and reducing cardiac load during subsequent training sessions or competitive events, contributing positively to recovery and adaptation strategies.
