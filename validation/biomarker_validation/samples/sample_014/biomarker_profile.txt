Biomarker

EPB41 (Erythrocyte Membrane Protein Band 4.1)

Biomarker Group
---------------

Relevance for Sports
--------------------
9 / 10

Activity Context
----------------
The study investigated proteomic changes in collegiate athletes to identify novel diagnostic plasma biomarkers for sport-related concussion (SRC). Blood samples were collected from concussed and non-concussed collegiate athletes within 48 hours post-injury to diagnose SRC, with analyses conducted for acute (0-6 hours) and subacute (7-48 hours) phases.

Effect Summary
--------------
Plasma EPB41 levels were significantly more abundant (higher) in collegiate athletes after sport-related concussion (SRC) compared to non-concussed athletes. This increase was particularly pronounced more than 6 hours post-injury. Individually, plasma EPB41 showed an AUC of 0.956 for SRC diagnosis >6 hours post-injury, and its diagnostic accuracy reached an AUC of 1.000 when combined with alpha-synuclein (SNCA). EPB41 is an essential erythrocyte membrane component, stabilizing the spectrin-actin cytoskeleton, and also plays a role in the central nervous system.

Sport Implications
------------------
This biomarker offers a highly accurate plasma diagnostic tool for sport-related concussion (SRC) in athletes, especially when measured more than 6 hours post-injury and when combined with alpha-synuclein. Accurate and timely diagnosis of SRC is critical for athlete management, guiding appropriate recovery protocols, and ensuring safe return-to-play decisions. Monitoring EPB41 could objectively aid clinicians in identifying concussed athletes, thereby preventing premature return to sport, mitigating risks of secondary injury or prolonged symptoms, and optimizing the recovery and rehabilitation process, which are all fundamental to athlete well-being and performance longevity.
