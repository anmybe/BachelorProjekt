BIOMARKER PROFILE
==============================

Biomarker
---------
Early Quadriceps Relaxation Time

Biomarker Group
---------------
muscle_damage

Relevance for Sports
--------------------
7 / 10

Activity Context
----------------
Physically active older adults with low habitual protein intake participated in 12 weeks of walking exercise training for a 4-day walking event. They were randomized to receive either 31 g of daily milk protein concentrate or an iso-caloric placebo, alongside their training regimen which covered an average of 338-391 km over 12 weeks.

Effect Summary
--------------
Early quadriceps relaxation time was not significantly different between the protein supplementation group and the placebo group over 12 weeks of walking exercise training.

Sport Implications
------------------
Early quadriceps relaxation time is a key indicator of muscle relaxation kinetics, which is crucial for repetitive movements, efficient force production, and potentially injury prevention in athletes across various disciplines. In the context of this study involving physically active older adults undergoing walking exercise, protein supplementation did not provide additional benefits to muscle relaxation speed beyond those achieved from the exercise training itself. This suggests that for this specific population and exercise type, 31g/day of protein supplementation may not be a limiting factor or an effective strategy to further enhance quadriceps relaxation kinetics. The biomarker's utility lies in monitoring muscle fatigue and recovery, as impaired relaxation is often associated with muscle fatigue and reduced performance. The lack of change here indicates that the training and nutritional intervention did not specifically impact this aspect of muscle function differently between groups.
