Background In this post hoc analysis of the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES (Evaluation of Cardiovascular Outcomes After an Acute Coronary Syndrome During Treatment With Alirocumab) trial, we evaluated the efficacy of alirocumab in patients with probable heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH) or type III hyperlipoproteinemia (T3HLP). Methods Patients had clinical HeFH if baseline low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol was ≥250 mg/dL and the qualifying acute coronary syndrome event occurred before age 55 (men) or 60 years (women), or if baseline low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol was ≥330 mg/dL. Patients had T3HLP if the ratio, validated using the UK Biobank database, of non–high‐density lipoprotein cholesterol/apolipoprotein B was >2.6. Genetic variation affecting low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol levels (HeFH) and APOE genotypes (T3HLP) was assessed in a pharmacogenomics subgroup. Results In total, 5.8% of patients from ODYSSEY OUTCOMES and 5.9% from the pharmacogenomics subgroup qualified as having clinical HeFH. In the pharmacogenomics subgroup, 295 patients (2.5%) had genetically confirmed HeFH; 45.8% also qualified as having clinical HeFH. Lipid responses and clinical benefits of alirocumab were similar in all patients with or without HeFH. In the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES cohort, 30 of 18 924 patients (0.16%) were identified as having T3HLP. The pharmacogenomics subgroup included 13 of these 30 patients, of whom 7 (53.8%) were APOE2/2 homozygotes. Lipid responses to alirocumab in patients with T3HLP were comparable with those in the general population. In those with genetic dyslipidemia, adverse event incidence was similar to the overall group. Conclusions In ODYSSEY OUTCOMES, individuals with HeFH were common while those with T3HLP were rare. In their lipid responses, carriers of both conditions benefited from alirocumab. Our results demonstrate that alirocumab, a proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 monoclonal antibody, markedly reduces atherogenic lipid levels; our results also suggest reducing cardiovascular risk in patients with probable heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia or type III hyperlipoproteinemia. What Are the Clinical Implications? Marked reductions in atherogenic lipids were also seen in patients with type III hyperlipoproteinemia treated with alirocumab versus placebo. Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 monoclonal antibodies are already indicated for use in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia, but their use and utility in patients with type III hyperlipoproteinemia should also be recognized. PCSK9 (Proprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9) inhibiting monoclonal antibodies, which increase expression of the low‐density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR), effectively reduce low‐density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‐C) levels even in patients with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HeFH), whose LDLR function is diminished. 1 , 2 However, while outcome trials that confirmed the clinical benefits of the 2 available antibodies did not specifically target patients with HeFH, it is likely that many participants had this condition given the study entry requirement (LDL‐C >70 mg/dL on maximally tolerated, high‐potency lipid‐lowering therapy [LLT] and preexisting coronary artery disease). 3 , 4 Several useful diagnostic criteria have been developed for the clinical diagnosis of familial hypercholesterolemia (FH). 5 , 6 , 7 One of the main tools was developed by the Dutch Lipid Clinic Network (DLCN), which uses a scoring system (3–5, possible FH; 6–8, probable FH; and >8, definite FH) to predict the likelihood of FH in the absence of genetic information. 5 Because large trials do not usually collect much of the information needed for the DLCN score (ie, xanthomas, arcus, detailed family history, or genetic data), retrospective attribution of FH status must rely on the presence of LDL‐C levels (≥250–330 mg/dL [adds 5 points]; ≥330 mg/dL [adds 8 points]), 7 and the presence of premature coronary artery disease (adds 2 points). While it is difficult to extract off‐treatment data in those who have been receiving long‐term statin therapy, methods to back‐calculate off‐treatment LDL‐C levels based on type and dose of statin have been used and validated. 8 Type III hyperlipoproteinemia (T3HLP) is another genetic dyslipidemia often misdiagnosed as common hypercholesterolemia or combined hyperlipidemia. T3HLP develops due to a variant in the gene for apolipoprotein E (Apo E) ( APOE ), resulting in impaired processing and clearance of lipoprotein remnants, thus causing their accumulation in plasma. 9 The most common genetic basis is homozygosity for the variant of APOE called APOE2 , 9 although many sporadic cases have been reported as secondary to rare APOE variants in the heterozygous state. 10 , 11 , 12 Although several statins are approved by the US Food and Drug Administration for the treatment of T3HLP, 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 , 17 PCSK9 inhibitors have not yet produced evidence of benefits for this condition. Just like HeFH, T3HLP is more rigorously and accurately diagnosed with a genetic test but, unlike HeFH, its genotype only rarely produces dyslipidemia (because of low penetrance), and its phenotype is not easily identified solely on the basis of the standard lipid panel (often diagnosed as common combined dyslipidemia). Several biomarker levels and ratios have been adopted for the identification of T3HLP without the need for genetic testing, 18 , 19 , 20 , 21 although most are neither specific nor sensitive enough for a diagnosis without knowledge of the APOE genotype status. The ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trial was a phase 3, randomized, placebo‐controlled, multicenter study designed to compare the efficacy of alirocumab versus placebo on cardiovascular outcomes among patients with recent acute coronary syndrome (ACS) on intensive or maximally tolerated statin therapy. 4 A total of 18 924 patients were randomized, and the study met the primary end point of a 15% reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACEs) in the treatment group versus placebo (hazard ratio [HR], 0.85 [95% CI, 0.73–0.98]). In this post hoc analysis, we used established and novel biomarkers to identify patients from the ODYSSEY OUTCOMES trial who may have been affected by HeFH or T3HLP, and to determine the efficacy of alirocumab on their lipid parameters and, for HeFH, cardiovascular outcomes."
