Background and Objectives The significance of anti-contactin–associated protein 1 (Caspr1) antibodies in autoimmune nodopathies (ANs) has not been fully established. The aim of this study was to elucidate the clinical profiles of Caspr1 AN. Methods Consecutive serum samples were included from patients with chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP) nationwide who were referred to our laboratory for antibody testing with questionnaires as part of routine clinical practice and who fulfilled definite European Federation of Neurological Societies and Peripheral Nerve Society electrodiagnostic criteria. Anti-Caspr1 immunoglobulin (Ig) G was screened using in-house ELISA and confirmed using immunohistochemistry and Western blotting. Clinical data, electrophysiologic findings, and treatment responses were retrospectively collected. The pathologic features of sural nerves were also examined. Inflammatory Neuropathy Cause and Treatment (INCAT) disability scores, antibody titers, and serum neurofilament light chain (NfL) levels were analyzed at baseline and follow-up. Results Of 559 screened serum samples from patients with CIDP (median age at onset 54 years, 35% female), 19 cases with anti-Caspr1 IgG were identified; the main subclass was IgG4 (17 cases). Patients with IgG4 Caspr1 AN cases exhibited an older age at disease onset (median 67 years); male predominance (82%); high proportions of limb weakness (100%), gait disturbance (100%), tremor (65%), and sensory ataxia (82%); and very high CSF protein levels (249 mg/dL). Nerve conduction studies showed prolonged F-wave and distal motor latencies and reduced sensorimotor conduction velocities in all nerves tested. Light microscopy findings of sural nerve biopsy specimens from 4 patients with Caspr1 AN indicated a loss of myelinated fiber density and myelin ovoids without macrophage-mediated demyelination or onion bulbs. Electron microscopic evaluation demonstrated axo-glial detachment. Patients with IgG4 Caspr1 AN showed a poor response to IVIg (31%) and needed combined immunotherapies. Even after correcting for age, serum NfL levels were higher in patients with IgG4 Caspr1 AN than healthy controls ( p = 0.0609) and correlated with INCAT scores ( p = 0.0143). In the 7 patients with 2 consecutive serum samples, antibody titers decreased with clinical improvement. Discussion IgG4 Caspr1 AN presents with a similar clinical phenotype to other nodopathies (e.g., neurofascin 155 and contactin 1), but with an older age at onset.       "text": "Introduction Autoimmune nodopathies (ANs) are relatively newly proposed immune-mediated neuropathies; they are associated with disease-specific autoantibodies against cell adhesion molecules—such as neurofascin (NF) 186, NF155, contactin 1 (CNTN1), and contactin-associated protein 1 (Caspr1)—located in nodes of Ranvier and paranodes. 1-4 On the basis of their specific clinicopathologic characteristics, ANs were classified separately from chronic inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy (CIDP) in the European Academy of Neurology and Peripheral Nerve Society (EAN/PNS) 2021 guidelines. 4 Recent cohort studies have established the common clinical features of patients with immunoglobulin (Ig) G4 autoantibodies against NF155 and CNTN1; they are characterized by distal-dominant sensorimotor neuropathy, ataxia, highly elevated CSF protein levels, a poor response to IVIg, and a good response to rituximab. 5-12 Moreover, their sural nerve biopsy specimens show axo-glial detachment without macrophage-mediated demyelination, inflammatory cells, or onion bulbs. 13 Patients with anti-Caspr1 IgG (Caspr1 AN) were first described in 2016, and neuropathic pain and cranial nerve involvement were noted as possible specific features. 14,15 However, the clinical profile of Caspr1 AN has not yet been fully elucidated because of its low prevalence and a limited number of case series. 16-25 In this study, we retrospectively screened serum samples from the largest cohort of Japanese patients with CIDP to date, aiming to unveil the clinicolaboratory, electrophysiologic, and pathologic features of Caspr1 AN. Methods Participants Serum samples from patients with immune-mediated neuropathies at multiple medical institutions in Japan were sent to the Department of Neurology at Kyushu University for diagnostic tests of paranodal autoantibodies as part of routine clinical practice, with questionnaires including clinical, laboratory, and electrophysiologic data supplied by treating physicians. Of these, we enrolled consecutive serum samples from patients with CIDP fulfilling the definite European Federation of Neurological Societies (EFNS)/PNS electrodiagnostic criteria between October 2015 and December 2023 ( Figure 1 ). In addition, consecutive patients with electrodiagnostically definite CIDP who were followed at Kyushu University Hospital between April 2000 and December 2023 were included. We excluded patients with anti-NF155 IgG (NF155 AN) or without available samples. Screening for anti-CNTN1 IgG was also performed to evaluate double positivity for autoantibodies targeting CNTN1 and Caspr1. 18,26 Patients with anti-CNTN1 IgG were referred to as “CNTN1 AN,” and those with IgG against Caspr1 and CNTN1 were referred to as “Caspr1 and CNTN1 AN.” 26 Autoantibodies against NF155 or CNTN1 were measured using flow cytometric cell-based assay (CBA) with human embryonic kidney 293 cell lines stably expressing human NF155 or CNTN1, as previously described. 6,27 Two additional cases diagnosed as Caspr1 AN in Yamaguchi University 23 (patient 19) and Nagoya University (patient 20) were included for clinical characterization. Serum samples from both patients were referred to the Department of Neurology at Kyushu University to confirm antibody positivity. Serum samples from patients with other peripheral neuropathies, including Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS)/Fisher syndrome (n = 49); anti–myelin-associated glycoprotein neuropathy (n = 11); Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease (n = 9); hereditary neuropathy with liability to pressure palsies (n = 1); and polyneuropathy, organomegaly, endocrinopathy, M protein, and skin changes syndrome (n = 9), were used as disease controls. Serum samples of healthy controls (n = 32) were taken from a former study. 6 Clinical and laboratory data were obtained from a questionnaire and medical records. Nerve conduction studies (NCSs) were performed using standard procedures at each institution. We collected the following neurophysiologic data: motor nerve conduction and F-wave studies in the median, ulnar, and tibial nerves and sensory conduction studies in the median, ulnar, and sural nerves. Bilateral values were included when available. Baseline-to-peak amplitudes were measured for compound muscle action potentials. Concomitant diabetes mellitus was defined by hemoglobin A1c values ≥6.5% (national glycohemoglobin standardization program) or the use of glucose-lowering treatment. Regarding treatment response, a good response was defined as an improvement ≥1 point in the Inflammatory Neuropathy Cause and Treatment (INCAT) disability score. A partial response was defined as an improvement of neurologic symptoms, examined by treating neurologists, without achieving a 1-point improvement in the INCAT score.
